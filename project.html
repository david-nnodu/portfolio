The journey

Overview

Sante LIMS is a desktop app for Sante Diagnostics, a medical laboratory. It handles a lab's whole day: patients order tests, staff collect and process samples, results are uploaded and checked, and patients get an email when their results are ready.

Everyone logs in to the same app, but each person sees a different dashboard depending on their role. A Super Admin sets up the tests the lab offers and oversees everything. A Lab Attendant runs daily operations. A Customer orders tests and views their own results.

I built it as my examination project for CSC 310 at Pan-Atlantic University, working from a brief that set out the functional and technical requirements.

Why this route exists

The problem

Sante Diagnostics ran test requests, sample tracking and result delivery on paper. Results took longer than they should, records could get lost, and patients had no way to see where their test was without calling or visiting the lab.

The system needed to stand on three pillars: administrative governance (control over who can do what, and a record of every action), operational precision (every sample tracked from collection to validation), and customer transparency (patients see their requests, payment status and results for themselves).

What's inside the bus

Features

  • Three roles, three dashboards Super Admin, Lab Attendant and Customer each get their own screens and permissions after logging in.
  • Secure sign-up and login Passwords are hashed with BCrypt. Customers who register themselves confirm their email with a 6-digit code.
  • Forced password change Accounts created by staff start with a temporary password that must be changed on first login.
  • Custom test builder The admin defines each test's type, price, turnaround time and result format (numeric, text, PDF or image).
  • Request queue with payments Customers see the lab's bank details when they order. Staff check the transfer and mark the request Paid.
  • Sample tracking Attendants move each sample through Collected, Processing and Validated.
  • Result upload and verification PDF reports and images are attached to a request, and customers only see them after a staff member verifies them.
  • Live countdown and email alerts The customer dashboard counts down to each result's ready time, and the customer gets an email the moment a result is validated.
  • Audit trail Every key action, such as a sample status change, is written to a log that can only be added to, never edited or deleted.

Under the bonnet

Technologies used

  • Java 17 The core language, organised into controllers, DAO classes for database access, and services.
  • JavaFX + FXML The interface, with each screen and dashboard panel laid out in its own FXML file.
  • PostgreSQL Stores users, tests, requests, samples, results, email codes and the audit log.
  • jBCrypt Hashes passwords so they're never stored as plain text.
  • Jakarta Mail Sends verification codes and "result ready" emails over SMTP, on a background thread so the app never freezes.
  • Ant The build tool, through NetBeans.

What the road taught me

What I learned

Roles shape the whole design

Deciding early what each role can see and do made everything else easier. Separate dashboards and panels per role kept the screens simple and stopped customers from reaching staff tools.

Keep slow work off the UI thread

Sending an email takes a few seconds. Running it on a background thread meant the app stayed responsive while the message went out.

Security is in the small decisions

Hashing passwords with BCrypt, forcing a password change for staff-created accounts, and making the audit log add-only taught me to think about trust and accountability, not just features.

Never hard-code secrets

Database and email passwords live in a config file that stays off GitHub, with an example file showing what to fill in.
